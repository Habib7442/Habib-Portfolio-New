The Verdict
No, you can't make a Google Business Profile — and even if you could, it wouldn't do what you want. The right tool is structured data, not a Maps listing.
01 — Why GBP doesn't fit

Two reasons it's the wrong tool.

Google Business Profile was built for a specific kind of business — one with a storefront, or a service area you travel to. A fully remote freelancer is, by Google's own definition, not that. The deeper issue is that even a perfect listing wouldn't solve the problem you're actually trying to solve.

i.

Eligibility, by Google's own rule.

Google's policy reads: "To qualify for a Business Profile, your business must make in-person contact with customers during its stated hours." A fully remote practice that never meets clients face-to-face fails the test. Profiles that don't qualify get suspended — and recovering one is a long, frustrating road.

ii.

It solves the wrong problem.

GBP is built for local discovery — "web designer near me" searches on Google Maps. It's not what ranks a person for their name. Personal name-search is governed by a different system entirely: entity SEO and the Knowledge Graph. Different fuel, different engine.

02 — Current state

You're already winning the search.

Before plotting next moves, it's worth marking where you stand. A search for your name returns habibfolio.tech in the first position. Your LinkedIn and GitHub fill out page one. The discovery problem is, in large part, already solved.

The opportunity isn't ranking — it's earning a Knowledge Panel.

The Goalpost · Phase Two

The next tier of personal-brand SEO is the Knowledge Panel — that sidebar card on Google with your photo, role, links, and bio that appears for established public identities. It's awarded, not bought, and it's driven by one specific signal more than any other: structured data telling Google these accounts are the same entity.

04 — The snippet

The fifteen-minute move that changes everything.

Drop this into your site's <head> — ideally in your Next.js root layout so it appears on every page. The sameAs array is the critical line: it's how Google fuses your portfolio, LinkedIn, GitHub, and Twitter into a single, recognized entity.

Schema.org · Person · JSON-LD → Drop into <head> 
<script type="application/ld+json">
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "Person",
  "name": "Habib Tanwir",
  "alternateName": "Habib Tanwir Laskar",
  "url": "https://www.habibfolio.tech",
  "image": "https://www.habibfolio.tech/og.png",
  "jobTitle": "Engineer & Designer",
  "description": "Full-stack engineer and designer building AI-powered SaaS products and brand identity.",
  "knowsAbout": ["TypeScript", "Next.js", "Supabase", "React Native", "AI Orchestration", "Brand Identity"],
  "worksFor": {
    "@type": "Organization",
    "name": "Flerid Technologies"
  },
  "sameAs": [
    "https://github.com/Habib7442",
    "https://in.linkedin.com/in/habib-tanwir",
    "https://twitter.com/TanwirHabib"
  ]
}
</script>

After deploying, validate with Google's Rich Results Test — paste the URL, confirm the Person schema is detected.

05 — One edge case

The narrow window where GBP could work.

Exception

If you ever maintain a real address clients could visit.

A coworking space you actually use, a studio you rent, a home office you'd open to a visiting collaborator — Google allows a "service area business" listing in those cases. For a name-search goal, though? Still the wrong tool. Knowledge Panels are downstream of structured data, not Maps pins. Skip it.

Seven moves, in order of leverage.

Top of the list is the only one that's truly urgent. The rest compound over weeks and months — finish them when you have a quiet afternoon. The total time investment is under two hours.

    Ship the Schema.org Person JSON-LD

    Impact Very high
